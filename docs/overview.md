# What is AI Jury?

AI Jury is a web platform for competition, hackathon and pitch-session organisers who need to judge many pitch decks consistently. An organiser creates a project, adds participants and their pitch decks, and starts judging. AI Jury evaluates every submission with an AI panel that simulates 11 judge roles — CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO, CRO, CHRO, CLO, CAO, CSO and CPO — and returns a leaderboard with a detailed, per-judge report for each participant.

## Who it's for

Organisers of hackathons, pitch competitions, accelerator demo days and internal innovation contests, who currently judge submissions one of two ways:

- **Manually**, with a panel of human judges and a spreadsheet — slow, inconsistent between judges, and hard to give every participant written feedback.
- **With a single generic AI score**, from a tool that runs one model over each deck and returns one number, without the reasoning a founder or a judging committee can act on.

AI Jury replaces the first with software and improves on the second by scoring from 11 distinct role perspectives instead of one.

## How judging works

1. **Create a project** and add participants, each with a pitch deck (PDF or slide file).
2. **Start judging.** AI Jury evaluates every deck against all 11 judge roles.
3. **Get a leaderboard.** Participants are ranked with an overall score.
4. **Read the per-judge report.** Each of the 11 judges contributes its own scored breakdown, in an expandable card, from its own professional lens — for example the CFO role checks whether the numbers in the deck are internally consistent, and the CSO role adds recommendations.

A mock mode is available for trying the product without spending on AI evaluation.

## How it differs from the alternatives

| | Manual + spreadsheet | Single-score AI tools | AI Jury |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reads every deck | Yes, if judges have time | Yes | Yes |
| Consistent standard across judges | No — drifts between reviewers | Yes | Yes |
| Feedback depth | Whatever judges write, if anything | One score, little reasoning | 11 role-specific scored reports per participant |
| Time for a large batch | Days to weeks | Minutes | Minutes |

Incumbent awards platforms have started adding AI scoring on top of their existing submission tools; what they add is typically a single configurable AI field rather than a panel of distinct judge roles reading every deck from a different angle.
