# AI Jury vs Award Force and other AI-assisted judging tools

If you are evaluating AI-assisted judging software for a competition, hackathon or pitch event, you have likely already seen Award Force, and possibly Sopact or a live-scoring tool like ScoreJudge. Here is the actual difference, stated plainly.

## The short answer

Award Force and Sopact both add an AI *field* to an existing submissions-and-scoring workflow: one configurable AI assessment attached to an entry, alongside your human judges' scores. AI Jury's AI panel *is* the judging step — it reads every deck from 11 distinct, named professional roles (CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO, CRO, CHRO, CLO, CAO, CSO, CPO) and returns a separate scored report from each one, not a single AI-assisted number.

## Comparison

| | Award Force | Sopact | AI Jury |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Full awards-management platform (submissions, judging, payments, reporting) with an AI field type added to entries | Entry review and scoring platform with AI rubric scoring | Judging tool purpose-built for pitch decks, scored by an 11-role AI panel |
| AI judging shape | One configurable AI field per entry, alongside human judge scores | AI reads each entry against a rubric and drafts one score with quoted evidence | 11 named judge roles, each producing its own scored, written report per deck |
| Judging modes | Four modes (Qualifying, Top Pick, Voting, VIP), sequential or parallel rounds | Rubric-based AI review with human confirmation | Single AI-panel judging run per deck |
| Best fit | Large-scale awards programs already running Award Force's full submissions pipeline | Award programs that want AI-assisted rubric scoring with a human in the loop | Organisers who want deep, multi-perspective, written feedback per pitch deck, fast |

*Award Force and Sopact feature descriptions above are drawn from their own public marketing pages, current as of 2026‑09‑20 — see sources. They are not independently verified beyond what those pages state, and pricing is not compared here because none is published on either page.*

## Why 11 roles instead of one AI field or one AI score

A single AI field or a single AI score gives you one number and, at best, one paragraph of reasoning. A founder or a judging committee asking "why did we score this way" for a specific submission often needs more than one lens: is the *market* claim credible, are the *numbers* internally consistent, is the *team* claim backed by the deck. AI Jury's 11 roles exist to answer exactly that — each one scores and writes from its own professional angle, so the report reads like notes from 11 different reviewers instead of one blended verdict.
